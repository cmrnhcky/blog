<span class="lede">Everybody I know</span> who is good with money is boring about it, and everybody I know who is interesting about money is broke. ▶ Open by stating the claim flatly. No throat-clearing, no "there's been a lot of talk lately."

▶ **Argue the losing side first, and argue it properly.** A paragraph that genuinely makes the case for the thing you are about to reject. This is the entire difference between a Greater Than and a rant. If the reader cannot tell which side you are on by the end of this paragraph, you have done it right.

▶ **The turn.** One sentence. Not a paragraph — a sentence. It is the hinge the piece swings on and it should be findable at a glance.

▶ Then two or three paragraphs of the actual case, in descending order of strength. Every claim here has to survive a reader who holds the other position and knows the field better than you.

> ▶ A quote from someone who is not you, if one earns its place. It should carry a fact or a formulation you cannot better — never a decoration, and never a substitute for making the point yourself.

▶ Land it. **Unhedged.** "It depends" is not a verdict, it is a refusal to file one, and a Greater Than with no verdict is a topic.
