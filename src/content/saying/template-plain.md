     NO FORM — the escape hatch

     Note what is missing from the frontmatter above: there is no
     `form:` line. That is legal and deliberate. Topic is required
     because it is the navigation; form is optional because not every
     piece is a franchise.

     Use this when:
     - The piece is a scene, not an argument
     - You are writing about something you have not concluded yet
     - Forcing it into Endorsements or Greater Than would make it
       dishonest

     Fatherhood pieces will mostly land here. So will anything that
     is really just a good paragraph you wanted to keep.

     THE ONLY RULE: it still has to end somewhere. A piece with no
     form has no scaffolding to lean on, which makes the ending the
     entire job. Write the last line first if you have to.

     LENGTH: 200-600 words. Short is fine. "We will write to write
     and categorize when finished" — that is the whole point of this
     template existing.

▶ OPEN IN THE MIDDLE OF SOMETHING. A time, a place, an action. Not a thesis. "It is 4:40 and the house is still dark" beats "I have been thinking about early mornings."

▶ THE BODY. Whatever it actually is. This form has no moves to hit, which is the freedom and the difficulty.

▶ ONE PLACE WHERE YOU STEP BACK. Exactly one. The temptation in a formless piece is to keep zooming out until it becomes a lesson. Resist past the first time.

▶ THE ENDING. Not a summary and not a moral. Ideally the same register you opened in — if you started with a scene, end with one.
