     ENDORSEMENTS — the shape

     1. Name the thing and its price in the first sentence. No warm-up,
        no "I've been thinking a lot about." Just the object.
     2. Concede what it is not. This is where credibility is bought.
        A piece that only praises reads as an ad.
     3. Verdict early and in bold. Don't make anyone scroll for it.
     4. One paragraph on the principle underneath. This is why someone
        who does not care about the object keeps reading — and it is
        the only paragraph that is actually about you.
     5. Land on a line that survives being quoted alone.

     LENGTH: 250-400 words. "The $13 Whiskey" is 225 and works fine.
     Long is not the goal. Finished is.

▶ OPEN WITH THE OBJECT AND THE NUMBER. The whiskey piece opens "Evan Williams Black Label. Shelf price: $13." Two fragments. That is the whole move — the reader knows what this is about before the second line.

▶ THEN THE LINE THAT ADMITS THE OBVIOUS. Something like: you have walked past it a hundred times. Or: everyone already owns one of these. Say the thing the reader is thinking so they stop thinking it.

▶ NOW CONCEDE. I am not here to tell you it is the best ▶ THING ▶ ever made. It is not. What it is: ▶ THE HONEST CLAIM, which should be smaller and more specific than the hype around it.

**The MVP rating: ★★★★☆ — Would buy at full price.**

Which is to say: ▶ THE PROOF THAT YOU MEAN IT. Not an argument, a behavior. "I do buy it at full price, repeatedly, when bottles that cost four times as much sit within arm's reach." What you actually do is more persuasive than what you claim.

▶ THE PRINCIPLE PARAGRAPH. Zoom out exactly once. The whiskey piece goes: the instinct to spend more because more must mean better is the same instinct that makes you a bad bettor, a bad shopper, and a bad judge of anything that matters. One move, one paragraph. Do not write three.

▶ THE LAST LINE. Short. Quotable. Ends the argument rather than summarizing it. "A $13 whiskey that drinks like $40 is not a compromise. It is a win."
