<span class="lede">He is four</span>, so the question comes without warning and in the middle of something else. ▶ Open with the moment. No setup, no "lately I've been thinking about." The reader can catch up.

▶ Let the second paragraph stay in the scene. The temptation in a fatherhood piece is to reach for the meaning on the second beat. Don't. Meaning that arrives that fast was decided before the scene happened, and it reads that way.

> ▶ If a child said something, put it here and put it here exactly. Do not improve it. The hanging quotation mark gives a four-year-old's sentence the same typographic weight the site gives Oscar Wilde, and that joke is worth the accent all by itself.

▶ A paragraph on what you did with it. What you said back, or did not say back. Specific.

▶ Now step back — once, briefly, and without announcing it. The strongest version of this paragraph does not contain the words "as a father" or "I realised."

▶ Land on the concrete, not the abstract. Come back to the room, the time of day, the thing in his hand. The reader will do the rest, and what the reader does themselves is the only part that stays with them.
